# M-104 Cooling System Troubleshooting and Safety Reference

> **Document status:** General troubleshooting reference for a synthetic hackathon environment. This is not a verified manufacturer-issued standard operating procedure (SOP). Confirm machine configuration, approved inspection steps, and safety requirements against manufacturer documentation and site procedures before applying this guidance to real equipment.

## Purpose

This reference describes possible cooling-system investigation paths for M-104, represented in this project as a hydraulic press.

For this demonstration, the cooling system is assumed to be associated with machine temperature and may be relevant when temperature changes occur alongside vibration, power consumption, or hydraulic pressure changes.

These relationships are investigation hypotheses, not verified diagnoses of physical equipment.

## Operating Temperature Reference

Verified manufacturer temperature limits are not available in this knowledge base.

Compare temperature trends with nearby readings, comparable operating conditions, sensor identity, and relevant machine context. Consult approved machine documentation before determining whether a temperature is within an acceptable operating range.

Do not treat illustrative values, historical observations, or model predictions as manufacturer-approved operating limits.

## Possible Cooling-System Warning Signs

The following observations may justify further investigation:

- Increasing machine temperature
- Increasing vibration
- Changes in apparent cooling performance
- Changes in power consumption
- Changes in hydraulic pressure

These observations do not independently establish cooling-system failure. Confirm sensor meaning, measurement context, and other available evidence before proposing a root-cause hypothesis.

## Cooling-System Investigation

When temperature trends change, consider reviewing the following evidence where it is available and relevant:

1. Confirm the temperature sensor identity, timestamp, and operating context.
2. Compare the temperature trend with recent observations under comparable conditions.
3. Review available cooling-system alarms and maintenance records.
4. Check available records or approved inspection results relating to coolant circulation, cooling-pump operation, coolant level, and cooling lines.
5. Determine whether vibration, power consumption, or hydraulic pressure changed during the same period.
6. Document supporting evidence, conflicting evidence, and information that is unavailable.

Physical inspections must be performed by appropriately qualified personnel using approved equipment-specific procedures and safety requirements. This reference does not authorize an inspection or specify a mandatory inspection sequence.

## Combined Symptoms

When temperature increases alongside vibration or apparent degradation in cooling performance, review the timing and relationship of these observations.

Possible investigation paths include:

- Cooling-pump performance
- Coolant circulation and cooling lines
- Relevant component condition
- Machine operating state and load
- Related hydraulic-system behavior
- Previous maintenance records and incidents

Historical cooling-pump problems may provide useful comparisons when relevant records are available. Similar symptoms do not prove that the current event has the same cause.

Do not conclude that the cooling pump has failed without supporting evidence.

## Escalation and Safety

If temperature continues to increase or several sensor signals show persistent or worsening abnormalities:

1. Review the available sensor trends, machine state, and alarms.
2. Notify qualified maintenance personnel according to site procedures.
3. Review the approved machine documentation and relevant maintenance history.
4. Follow the applicable manufacturer instructions and site safety procedures to determine the appropriate response.

Decisions about reducing machine load, continuing operation, or initiating a controlled shutdown must follow approved machine and site procedures. If an immediate safety risk exists, follow the approved site emergency procedures.

Do not bypass guards, interlocks, or other safety systems. Do not perform maintenance on energized equipment unless explicitly permitted by an approved procedure.

## Preventive Maintenance

Cooling-system maintenance should follow the verified manufacturer maintenance schedule and approved site procedures.

When reviewing maintenance records, consider whether they document:

- Cooling-pump condition and servicing
- Coolant circulation and relevant lines
- Reported temperature abnormalities
- Related alarms and sensor trends
- Previous corrective actions and subsequent observations

A record of suspected pump wear should be treated as historical evidence, not proof that the pump is responsible for a new anomaly.

## Limitations and Evidence Requirements

- This reference is project-authored troubleshooting guidance, not a verified OEM SOP.
- No numerical temperature or other operating limits are established here.
- Suspected causes must remain hypotheses until supported by evidence.
- Missing specifications, sensor details, and maintenance records must be reported as unknown rather than inferred.
- Verified manufacturer documentation and approved site safety procedures take precedence over this reference.
